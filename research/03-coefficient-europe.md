# Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy): Origins, Funders, the Stripe/Collison Network, and European Activities

## TL;DR
- **Coefficient Giving is the new name of Open Philanthropy** — a rebrand announced November 18, 2025 — founded by Dustin Moskovitz and Cari Tuna (via Good Ventures) together with Holden Karnofsky/GiveWell, now led by CEO Alexander Berger. The rebrand signals a strategic shift from serving one anchor donor to running ~13 multi-donor "funds" that other philanthropists can join.
- **Newer donors are confirmed and documented**: Stripe co-founder/CEO Patrick Collison (Abundance & Growth Fund), Lucy/Lucinda Southworth (wife of Google's Larry Page), and Bill Gates/Gates Foundation are among the new backers. The Collison/Stripe/progress-studies overlap is real and documented (shared personnel, co-funded metascience, the Institute for Progress, co-founding the Arc Institute) — but Collison is a fund contributor and network ally, not a Coefficient board member, and Stripe as a company does not fund it.
- **Europe is a meaningful but mostly indirect theatre**: Coefficient has a London coworking hub and UK/Europe-based staff (e.g., Aveek Bhattacharya, ex-UK Treasury), funds UK/EU AI-governance and farm-animal-welfare grantees (BlueDot Impact $25.6M; Centre for Long-Term Resilience ~£4M; Eurogroup for Animals €5.5M), and Dylan Matthews (ex-Vox *Future Perfect*) joined in December 2025 — but his remit is the US-focused Abundance & Growth Fund, not Europe.

---

## Key Findings

**On identity and rebrand.** On November 18, 2025, Open Philanthropy announced it had become "Coefficient Giving." The mission is unchanged; the rebrand reframes the organization as a philanthropic funder *and* advisor serving multiple donors, not just Good Ventures. On the name, CEO Alexander Berger told Vox's Bryan Walsh that "coefficient is a multiplier": the "co-" nods to collaboration with other givers, and the "efficient" is a reminder of the north star of effectiveness — while a coefficient mathematically multiplies the value of whatever it is paired with. Two practical drivers: the organization was routinely confused with OpenAI and the Open Society Foundations (journalists thought they were part of OpenAI; potential grantees thought they were from Open Society), and leadership felt "Open Philanthropy" no longer fit an org increasingly working with many donors.

**On leadership.** Alexander Berger is co-founder and CEO. He co-founded the project in 2011 (as GiveWell Labs), was the first employee of the independent organization in 2017, became co-CEO in 2021, and sole CEO in 2023. Cari Tuna is Chair (her title changed from a more operational role, though her responsibilities are unchanged). Holden Karnofsky co-founded GiveWell (2007) with Elie Hassenfeld and co-founded Open Philanthropy in 2014; he was CEO then co-CEO until 2023, took a leave to work on AI safety, and joined Anthropic in January 2025 — so he is no longer at Coefficient. Emily Oehlsen was promoted to President and now leads AI and biosecurity work; Otis Reid is Managing Director for Global Health & Wellbeing; Derek Hopf is Managing Director of Operations; Naina Bajekal (ex-TIME executive editor) is Director of Communications; Lewis Bollard leads farm animal welfare; Matt Clancy is Program Director of the Abundance & Growth Fund.

**On funders.** Good Ventures (Moskovitz/Tuna; ~$10 billion in assets) remains the primary funder and has committed over $4 billion. The strategic pivot is to bring in more donors: per Vox (November 2025), the group directed more than $100 million from donors beyond Good Ventures in 2024 and had already more than doubled that number in 2025; Particle reports outside donors pledged more than $200 million to Coefficient initiatives in 2025. Named new billionaire backers: **Patrick Collison** (Abundance & Growth Fund), **Lucy/Lucinda Southworth** (LEAF and Strep A funds), and **Bill Gates/Gates Foundation** (LEAF). Total 2025 giving crossed $1 billion for the first time.

**On origins.** 2011: Good Ventures + GiveWell launch GiveWell Labs. 2014: renamed Open Philanthropy Project. 2017: spun out as an independent LLC. ~2019: shortened to "Open Philanthropy." 2025: rebranded Coefficient Giving. The core method — "strategic cause selection" (importance, neglectedness, tractability) and "hits-based giving" — carried through all iterations.

**On the Stripe/Collison connection.** This is documented and substantive, not merely network proximity:
- Patrick Collison is a **named contributor to the $120M Abundance & Growth Fund** (launched March 2025), which absorbs and expands Open Phil's prior housing (YIMBY) and innovation-policy work. Good Ventures committed $60M; the announcement states it is grateful "for support from Good Ventures, which has committed $60M, and from the other private funders who matched them," plus "a contribution from Patrick Collison, who helped launch the Progress Studies movement." Inside Philanthropy characterizes Collison's as "an undisclosed contribution"; the fund page lists donors as "Good Ventures · Patrick Collison · Private funders (x2)."
- Collison, Berger, and Coefficient economist Matt Clancy appear together in the "Metascience 101" podcast series produced with the Institute for Progress (IFP) — a Coefficient/Open Phil-funded think tank of which Coefficient was a **founding donor**.
- Both Coefficient (via Moskovitz/Tuna) and Collison are among the **founding donors of the Arc Institute** ($650M, launched 2021), and both sit in the broader Fast Grants / progress-studies / metascience network (Tyler Cowen, Patrick Hsu, Adam Marblestone, Schmidt Futures, Sloan Foundation).
- **Boundary of the claim:** The two orbits overlap ideologically on progress studies, metascience, and the "abundance" agenda, and there is specific co-funding. But Collison is a fund donor and network ally, not a governance figure: there is no documented Coefficient board or advisory seat for Patrick or John Collison, and no evidence Stripe as a company funds Coefficient.

**On Dylan Matthews.** Confirmed: Dylan Matthews left Vox (where he founded/led *Future Perfect*) and joined Coefficient Giving in **December 2025** as **Associate Program Officer on the Abundance & Growth Fund**. His work spans the fund's topics (innovation, housing, growth, social-safety-net) and is **US-centric, not Europe-focused**.

**On Europe.** There is no dedicated "European program" or European legal entity I could confirm, but there is real European activity:
- **Presence:** Coefficient has a **London coworking hub** (plus NYC; permanent offices are San Francisco and Washington DC). Staff are dispersed "from the Pacific U.S. through European time zones."
- **Europe-focused/based staff:** Aveek Bhattacharya (Strategy Fellow, Cause Prioritization; ex-UK Treasury civil servant and ex-Social Market Foundation director; writes mostly on UK policy). Oliver Kim (development economics) and others sit on Cause Prioritization.
- **AI governance in the UK/EU:** major grantees include **BlueDot Impact** (UK; $25.6M over three years in 2025) and the **Centre for Long-Term Resilience** (UK; ~£4M, October 2024, plus up to £3M match). Open Phil's AI Governance and Technical AI Safety RFPs are open to European applicants. The **Talos Network** (Brussels-based EU AI-policy fellowship) states the majority of its budget has historically come from Coefficient/Good Ventures.
- **Farm animal welfare in the EU:** Europe is Open Phil's most active welfare region — grantees include **Eurogroup for Animals** (Belgium), **Compassion in World Farming** (UK), **Animal Equality** (Germany/Italy/Spain/UK), **Equalia/Animal Welfare Observatory** (Spain), and the **Albert Schweitzer Foundation** (Germany).
- **Abundance/growth in Europe:** currently **US-focused**; the Abundance & Growth Fund is "primarily in the United States." International expansion is aspirational, not funded.
- **Lead exposure (LEAF):** LMIC-focused by design; no European grantees.

## Details

### Stage 1 — Who they are, origins, and funding

**The rebrand mechanics.** The decision followed a February 2025 leadership meeting that weighed a website refresh versus a full rebrand. A naming agency proposed "Coefficient Giving," unveiled internally at an October 2025 team retreat, with a full website relaunch by year-end. Communications Director Naina Bajekal wrote the explainer "The Story Behind Our New Name"; Berger wrote the strategy essay "Open Philanthropy Is Now Coefficient Giving." The ~13 former program areas became "funds" that outside donors can support at scale (Abundance & Growth, Navigating Transformative AI, Farm Animal Welfare, Biosecurity & Pandemic Preparedness, Science and Global Health R&D, Global Aid Policy, Lead Exposure Action Fund, Global Health & Development, Effective Giving & Careers, and others). Coefficient solicits donors giving over $250,000/year (via partnerwithus@coefficientgiving.org), and has a formal Partnerships team of ~10 people.

**Structural nuance.** InfluenceWatch notes that at the end of 2024 Cari Tuna formed a new nonprofit, "Open Philanthropy Advisors," and that the relationship between the original Open Philanthropy Project Fund coalition and the new brand was not fully clarified in the rebrand announcement. Good Ventures reports all its grantmaking is now conducted through its partnership with Coefficient Giving. US-based program staff are typically employed by "Coefficient Giving LLC," which is not a 501(c)(3).

**Funding scale and trajectory.** Over $4 billion directed since 2014 (some post-rebrand pages now say over $5 billion including 2025); over $1 billion in 2025 alone (the largest year in its history); non-Good-Ventures funding grew from over $100M (2024) to over $200M (2025). Good Ventures holds ~$10 billion in assets; Moskovitz/Tuna signed the Giving Pledge (2010, as the youngest couple to do so) and intend to spend down within their lifetimes. Notable multi-donor vehicles include the $125M Lead Exposure Action Fund (LEAF, 2024), the $120M Abundance & Growth Fund (2025), and the newer Strep A Vaccine Fund (targeting $200M; ~$140M raised at launch, with anchor funders including Adam and Abigail Winkel, Good Ventures, Lucinda Southworth, and the Patchwork Collective).

**The Collison/Stripe/progress-studies ecosystem — precise mapping.**
- *Documented financial tie:* Patrick Collison is a named, confirmed contributor to the $120M Abundance & Growth Fund (Good Ventures $60M + Collison and "private funders (x2)" matching; Collison's specific amount is undisclosed). Fund leadership: Matt Clancy (Program Director), Alex Armlovich (housing), Jordan Dworkin, Willow Latham-Proença, Dylan Matthews, Nisha Austin (Chief of Staff), Saloni Dattani (volunteer advisor).
- *Shared intellectual infrastructure:* Coefficient/Open Phil is a founding donor of the Institute for Progress; the "Metascience 101" series was co-produced with IFP and features Berger, Clancy, Collison, Cowen, Hsu, Marblestone, Heidi Williams, and Kelsey Piper. Clancy sits with Collison, Cowen, and Coefficient's Emily Oehlsen on the advisory committee of J-PAL's Science for Progress Initiative (funded by Open Phil, Schmidt Futures, and the Sloan Foundation).
- *Shared grantee/donor overlaps:* Moskovitz/Tuna and the Collison brothers are co-founding donors of the Arc Institute; the Fast Grants alumni network (Collison, Cowen, Hsu, Marblestone) heavily overlaps with Coefficient's metascience grantmaking.
- *Boundary of the claim:* The relationship is one of aligned networks and specific co-funding, not corporate or governance integration. There is no documented Coefficient board seat or advisory role for Patrick or John Collison, and no evidence that Stripe as a company funds Coefficient.
- *The American Prospect reporting* (June 2026) situates Moskovitz and Collison within a broader "abundance capital stack" of Silicon Valley donors funding YIMBY/abundance organizing (its cited internal document lists CA YIMBY funders as "Mark Zuckerberg, Dustin Moskovitz, Patrick Collison, Ken Duda"), and notes the two separate $120M abundance funds — Coefficient's Abundance & Growth Fund and Jennifer Pahlka's Recoding America Fund. (These figures come from a leaked fundraising memo; the Prospect notes some "committed capital" numbers are estimates, not active funding.)

### Stage 2 — Current and upcoming European activities

**Physical/organizational footprint.** London coworking hub; staff dispersed through European time zones; no confirmed standalone European legal entity or "European office" beyond the coworking hub. Job postings sometimes accommodate UK-based hires (relocation/visa stipends, though Coefficient notes it cannot itself sponsor UK visas), and roles frequently request UK/US-Pacific time overlap.

**People with a Europe nexus.**
- *Aveek Bhattacharya* — Strategy Fellow, Cause Prioritization team; joined September 2025 from the UK Treasury (excise-tax lead); former Director/Research Director of the Social Market Foundation; PhD (LSE). Writes mostly on UK policy and appears on the Global Health & Wellbeing / Global Health & Development fund pages.
- *Dylan Matthews* — Associate Program Officer, Abundance & Growth Fund (joined December 2025); US-focused remit.
- Other UK/Europe-adjacent staff work across Cause Prioritization and AI/biosecurity in-time-zone.

**Currently funded European initiatives (confirmed grants).**

*AI governance / GCR (UK/EU):*
- **BlueDot Impact** (UK) — exactly $25,649,888 over three years for general support (2025 grant, following August 2024 support); Global Catastrophic Risks Capacity Building. BlueDot's mission is "to build the workforce needed to safely navigate transformative AI."
- **Centre for Long-Term Resilience** (UK) — ~£4,000,000 (grantee-reported; October 2024) plus a pledge to match up to £3M 1:1 ("Open Philanthropy pledged to match every £1 from other donors with an additional £1"), which CLTR called a "crucial window of opportunity"; AI/biosecurity policy. CLTR submitted written evidence to the EU's public consultation informing the European Biotech Act (November 2025).
- **Talos Network / Talos Institute** (Brussels EU-AI-policy fellowship) — Coefficient/Good Ventures has historically supplied the majority of Talos's budget (no confirmed figure); Open Phil helped launch the underlying EU Tech Policy Fellowship, which places fellows at CEPS, The Future Society, Centre for Future Generations, OECD.AI, the EU AI Office, and others.
- The Future Society, SaferAI, Pour Demain, Centre for Future Generations, RAND Europe, Ada Lovelace Institute, and CEPS — named EU AI-governance actors and/or Talos placement hosts, but no confirmed Coefficient grant amounts/dates (likely a reflection of the deprecated database rather than definitive absence).

*Farm animal welfare (EU) — Coefficient is described as the world's largest funder of farm animal welfare:*
- **Eurogroup for Animals** (Belgium) — a grant of €5,500,000 (~$5,699,748) over three years for farm animal welfare; plus €1,632,496 (fish welfare, 2024); €1,233,318 (EU trade strategy, 2024, amount updated July 2025); €875,745 (~$944k, chicken welfare advocacy, 2024); and earlier grants (€830,875; €586,000; a €13,242 Brussels broiler-advocacy convening).
- **Compassion in World Farming** (UK) — ~£2,728,437 (~$3.38M) over two years (date not confirmed as 2024–2026).
- **Animal Equality** (Germany/Italy/Spain/UK) — multiple grants including $2,110,460 (four grants) to expand EU work, plus $3M "exit grants" and $1,901,000 (four grants) for cage-free/broiler welfare (some likely predate 2024).
- **Equalia / Animal Welfare Observatory** (Spain) — €2,374,494 (~$2,409,106) over two years.
- **Albert Schweitzer Foundation** (Germany) — $3,000,000 over two years for general support.
- **L214** (France) — grant page exists (EU farm animal welfare legislation); amount not public post-rebrand.

*Biosecurity / EU Biotech Act:* No confirmed EU-Biotech-Act-specific Coefficient grants. The European Commission published its Biotech Act proposal in December 2025 (following an August–November 2025 consultation with biosecurity provisions); CLTR (a Coefficient grantee) engaged that consultation, making biosecurity the most plausible near-term EU-policy channel for Coefficient-funded influence.

*Abundance/housing in Europe:* None confirmed; the Abundance & Growth Fund is "primarily in the United States" (grantees include California YIMBY, Open New York, the Institute for Progress, the Center for Building in North America). International expansion is forward-looking/speculative.

*Lead exposure (LEAF):* LMIC-focused by design ($104M+ raised; partners include Good Ventures, the Gates Foundation, the ELMA Foundation, Alpha Epsilon Fund, the Livelihood Impact Fund, Lucinda Southworth, and the 10x Better Foundation). Per Particle, LEAF has disbursed about $20 million so far, including a $17 million grant to Pure Earth to curb lead exposure in India and other countries. No European grantees.

**Signals of European intent.**
- London coworking hub + willingness to hire/relocate UK-based staff.
- Recruiting team attended conferences in Nigeria, Mexico, Brazil, and India in 2025 (global sourcing, not specifically Europe).
- Repeated framing that "a group working on European policy benefits from having identifiably European funders," which Open Phil says it addresses by *advising other (European) donors* rather than making centralized grants — implying European grantmaking is often channeled through advised/partner donors and thus under-represented in the public database.
- The public grants database is being deprecated post-rebrand, so recent (2025–2026) European grants are increasingly hard to see publicly.

## Recommendations

**For a reader tracking Coefficient's European advance:**
1. **Watch the advised-donor channel, not just the grants database.** Coefficient explicitly says European policy grantees benefit from European funders, so it advises European donors rather than centralizing grants; the public database understates European activity. The signal that would change the picture: an announcement of a European pooled fund or a Brussels/London *program* (not just coworking).
2. **Track farm animal welfare and AI governance as the two live European beachheads.** These are where confirmed euro/pound-denominated grants already flow (Eurogroup for Animals, CIWF, Animal Equality, BlueDot, CLTR). New RFP cycles (AI Governance; Biosecurity, with EOIs open to May 11, 2026) are the most likely near-term vehicles for new European grants.
3. **Treat the Abundance & Growth Fund as US-first.** Dylan Matthews's hire and Collison's funding are US-centric. A European abundance/housing grant (e.g., to a UK YIMBY group such as Britain Remade) would be the tell that the fund is internationalizing; none exists yet.
4. **On the Collison/Stripe question, be precise.** Report Collison as a *fund donor and progress-studies co-traveler* — co-founding donor of the Arc Institute alongside Moskovitz/Tuna and co-participant in Coefficient-funded metascience — not as a board member or Stripe-corporate funder.
5. **Monitor the EU Biotech Act.** With the Commission's proposal published December 2025 and CLTR already engaging, biosecurity is the most plausible next EU-policy area for Coefficient-funded influence.

**Thresholds that would change these recommendations:** a named European entity/legal registration; a Brussels or London *office* with a country/regional director; a European-donor pooled fund; or a Europe-specific program-lead hire.

## Caveats
- **Database deprecation:** Post-rebrand, individual Open Phil grant pages redirect to fund landing pages, and the searchable per-grant database is being retired. Several European grant amounts/dates are sourced from grantee sites or Good Ventures' database rather than a current Coefficient primary page; some dates (e.g., CIWF, several Animal Equality grants) are not confirmed as 2024–2026.
- **Advised vs. directed funds:** Coefficient's database "generally does not include funding advised from non-Good-Ventures donors," so European (and other) activity is understated.
- **Speculative/unconfirmed items:** Abundance & Growth international expansion is aspirational; the Talos funding figure is unconfirmed; grants to The Future Society, SaferAI, Pour Demain, and Centre for Future Generations are unconfirmed (indeed, CFG's public funders are the Dreamery and Tanka Foundations, not Coefficient); the American Prospect's "capital stack" figures derive from a leaked memo and include estimates.
- **Source hygiene:** Grokipedia/LongtermWiki/Particle-type aggregators were used only for corroboration; primary figures rely on coefficientgiving.org, openphilanthropy.org, goodventures.org, Forbes, Bloomberg, Vox, and Inside Philanthropy, plus grantee sites.
- Holden Karnofsky's current role is at Anthropic (since January 2025), not Coefficient; his Coefficient role is historical.
