# The Collison/Stripe Ecosystem: A Network Map of Wealth, Science, Ideas, and Policy

## TL;DR
- **Patrick and John Collison — the Irish brothers who built Stripe (valued at $159B in February 2026; ~$17.5B each per Forbes 2026) — have converted their wealth and unusual convening power into one of the most coherent private intellectual empires in the world**, spanning frontier biology (Arc Institute), rapid science funding (Fast Grants), the Progress Studies movement (Works in Progress, Stripe Press), carbon removal (Frontier), and abundance-agenda policy — a network that now interlocks financially and personally with the Open Philanthropy/Coefficient Giving effective-altruism world.
- **Patrick did NOT step down as CEO in 2025 — this is the single most important factual correction.** He remains Stripe's CEO; the 2025 "change" was that he *added* a Meta board seat (effective April 15, 2025) and testified to Congress. John is President; Will Gaybrick is President of Product & Business; Claire Hughes Johnson is corporate advisor. There is no co-CEO.
- **The ecosystem is genuinely substantive but not immune to the charge of being a branding exercise for a deregulatory politics.** Arc has produced real *Nature* papers and open AI models; Frontier has committed $1.8B to carbon removal; Progress Ireland has moved Irish political debate. Yet the abundance/progress project draws credible left critique as billionaire-driven neoliberalism, and its founding metascience thesis ("science is slowing") remains academically contested.

## Key Findings

**Stripe's scale.** Per CNBC (Feb 24, 2026), "Stripe valued at $159 billion after tender offer for employees, shareholders" — up from $106.7B (Sept 2025) and $91.5B (Feb 2025), with Thrive Capital, Coatue, and a16z participating; total payment volume hit $1.9 trillion in 2025, up 34%. Valuation arc: $9.2B (2016) → $35B (2019) → $95B peak (2021) → ~$50B down-round (2023) → $65B (2024) → $91.5B (Feb 2025) → $106.7B (Sept 2025) → $159B (Feb 2026). Stripe is "robustly profitable" and repeatedly frames an IPO as "a solution in search of a problem." Dual HQ: South San Francisco and Dublin.

**Science/metascience.** Arc Institute (launched Dec 2021, $650M+), Fast Grants (2020, ~$50M), and the Progress Studies canon (2018 Collison–Nielsen and 2019 Collison–Cowen Atlantic essays) form a tightly linked lineage — all three Arc founders (Patrick Collison, Silvana Konermann, Patrick Hsu) first collaborated on Fast Grants.

**Media/ideas.** Stripe Press (books), Works in Progress magazine (acquired 2022), and Frontier (carbon removal AMC) are corporate vehicles for the founders' intellectual agenda.

**EA/Open Phil links.** Per Coefficient Giving's own announcement: "We're also grateful for a contribution from Patrick Collison, who helped launch the Progress Studies movement" to the $120M Abundance & Growth Fund. Per Forbes (Nov 18, 2025), "New billionaire funders include Stripe cofounder Patrick Collison, Lucy Southworth… and Bill Gates."

**Criticism.** The American Prospect (June 12, 2026) published leaked documents describing a "$260 million annually" billionaire "Abundance Capital Stack," quoting a fundraising memo that CA YIMBY's funding "largely originates from 'tech founders: Mark Zuckerberg, Dustin Moskowitz, Patrick Collison, Ken Duda [who] are 80%+ of the funding.'"

## Details

### Part 1 — The Collisons and Stripe

**Origins.** Patrick Collison was born 9 September 1988 in Limerick and raised in the village of Dromineer, Co. Tipperary, the eldest of three sons of microbiologist Lily and electronic engineer Denis Collison. He took his first computer course at the University of Limerick at age eight and won the 41st Young Scientist and Technology Exhibition in January 2005 (aged 16) with "Croma," a LISP-type programming language — a prize presented by President Mary McAleese. He and John founded software company Shuppa in Limerick in 2007; when Enterprise Ireland declined to fund it, they moved to Y Combinator, merged with Oxford graduates Harjeet and Kulveer Taggar to form Auctomatic, and sold it to Live Current Media in March 2008 for ~$5M — making them teenage millionaires. Patrick attended MIT (mathematics) and dropped out in 2009; John attended Harvard and also dropped out. In April 2022 Patrick married Swiss-American biochemist and Stanford professor Silvana Konermann, whom he met at the 2004 EU Young Scientist competition and with whom he co-founded the Arc Institute.

**Stripe's growth.** Founded 2010; in 2011 it raised $2M including from Elon Musk, Peter Thiel, Sequoia, a16z, and SV Angel. The brothers became the world's youngest self-made billionaires in November 2016 when a CapitalG/General Catalyst round valued the company at $9.2B. Stripe's 2025 annual letter reports it serves half the Fortune 100, 80% of the Forbes Cloud 100, and 78% of the Forbes AI 50; Stripe Atlas has incorporated roughly a quarter of new Delaware corporations. The company laid off ~14% of staff (1,100+ people) in November 2022 — Patrick took public responsibility, admitting the company "overhired" during the pandemic — and cut ~300 more (~3.5%) in early 2025.

**The 2025 crypto/stablecoin pivot (a defining strategic bet).** Stripe acquired Bridge, a stablecoin orchestration platform founded by ex-Coinbase executives Zach Abrams and Sean Yu, for $1.1B (announced Oct 2024, closed Feb 4, 2025) — the largest crypto M&A deal to that date, at roughly a 5.5x premium over Bridge's $200M August 2024 valuation. In July 2025 Stripe acquired Privy (110M+ programmable wallets); in September 2025 it unveiled Tempo, a payments-purpose-built Layer-1 blockchain co-incubated with Paradigm (Tempo reportedly raised a $500M Series A at a $5B valuation with Visa, Deutsche Bank, Nubank, and Revolut as partners, and launched on public testnet in December 2025). Stablecoin payment volume roughly doubled to ~$400B in 2025 (Stripe estimates ~60% B2B); Bridge's own volume more than quadrupled. In February 2026 Bridge received a conditional national trust bank charter from the OCC.

**Leadership (correcting the premise).** Patrick Collison remains co-founder and CEO. He did **not** step back in 2025. The genuine 2025 developments: he joined Meta's board effective April 15, 2025 (alongside Dina Powell McCormick — widely read as Meta courting the Trump administration ahead of its FTC antitrust trial), and he testified before the House Financial Services Committee in March 2025. John Collison is co-founder and President; Will Gaybrick is President of Product & Business; David Singleton is CTO; Claire Hughes Johnson (the former COO) is now corporate advisor. The brothers hold a controlling interest together.

**Wealth.** Forbes' 2026 World's Billionaires list places both brothers at ~$17.5B each (Patrick's net worth was cited at $17.5B as of June 2026). Note a large source discrepancy: Bloomberg's Billionaires Index estimates John nearer ~$9.3B, reflecting a more conservative ~12% stake calculation with a secondary-market discount. Use Forbes' figure but flag the divergence.

**John Collison specifically.** Beyond his President role, John is a licensed pilot and aviation enthusiast (he earned his private pilot's license as a teenager and owns Weston Airport near Dublin, Ireland's busiest general-aviation field by movements), and a pianist. He launched a podcast in 2025 titled **"A Cheeky Pint"** (Stripe cofounder interviews founders and leaders over a Guinness) — guests have included Elon Musk, Satya Nadella, Evan Spiegel, Tony Xu, Marc Andreessen, and Ben Thompson. He delivers data-heavy macroeconomics keynotes ("Indexing the economy") at Stripe Sessions, invoking economic history (Coase's theory of the firm). **Correction for the brief:** there is no John Collison series called "Cheque Depot" — the property is "A Cheeky Pint." And the celebrated "flip-the-script" Conversations with Tyler interview was conducted by *Patrick*, not John; a documented John Collison interview with Larry Summers or Ken Rogoff could not be verified (those names appear as *references within* the Cowen conversation).

**Stripe as an intellectual institution.** Stripe Press ("ideas for progress") publishes and reissues works aligned to the founders' worldview: Tyler Cowen's *Stubborn Attachments*, Donald Braben's *Scientific Freedom* (a direct influence on Arc's funding model), *The Dream Machine* (Waldrop), Richard Hamming's *The Art of Doing Science and Engineering*, *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (Munger), Elad Gil's *High Growth Handbook*, Martin Gurri's *The Revolt of the Public*, Byrne Hobart & Tobias Huber's *Boom*, and Stewart Brand's *Maintenance*. Stripe previously ran *Increment*, a developer/engineering magazine (now discontinued). And Stripe operates **Frontier** — see below.

### Part 2 — Science and Metascience Institutions

**Arc Institute (the flagship).** Launched December 15, 2021 as an independent nonprofit, co-founded by Patrick Collison, Silvana Konermann (Stanford, now Arc executive director), and Patrick Hsu (formerly Berkeley, now core Arc). It raised $650M+ from a donor list that is essentially the Collison/Moskovitz-Tuna nexus: Vitalik Buterin, Patrick and John Collison, the Ron Conway family, Crankstart (Michael Moritz & Harriet Heyman), Elad Gil and Jennifer Huang Gil, Daniel Gross, Dustin Moskovitz and Cari Tuna, Hemant and Jessica Taneja, plus Jane Street executives (Matt Berger, Craig Falls, Rob Granieri, James McClave, Adam Winkel).

Arc's structural philosophy is an explicit critique of the NIH grant system: Core Investigators receive renewable 8-year appointments with full salary and lab funding (labs of up to ~20 people), freeing them from perpetual grant-writing. Investigators may hold joint faculty appointments at Stanford, UC Berkeley, and UCSF. Arc also runs Technology Centers (machine learning, genome engineering, disease models, multiomics) and smaller instruments (Innovation Investigator awards, $100K Ignite Awards).

Scientific output has been real and high-profile: the **Evo / Evo 2 DNA foundation models** (Hsu and Brian Hie; Evo published in *Science* 2024, Evo 2 published in *Nature* after a February 2025 preprint, developed with NVIDIA and collaborators at Stanford, UCSF, UW, and Goodfire); **bridge RNA** programmable genome editing (Hsu lab, two back-to-back *Nature* papers, June 2024); and the **Virtual Cell Initiative** — the STATE model, the Virtual Cell Atlas (600M+ cells, including Tahoe-100M and scBaseCount), and the inaugural **Virtual Cell Challenge** (2025), which drew 1,200+ teams and 300+ final submissions. Arc partnered with NVIDIA (Jan 2025) and with 10x Genomics/Ultima (April 2025), and was named to The Audacious Project's 2025 cohort.

**Fast Grants (2020).** The COVID rapid-funding experiment, administered by the Mercatus Center and launched by Tyler Cowen, Patrick Collison, and Patrick Hsu. Applications took ~30 minutes; decisions came within ~two weeks (sometimes 48 hours); grants ran $10K–$500K. By April 2021 it had awarded 250+ grants totaling more than $50M, funding notably Yale's SalivaDirect saliva COVID test. Donors: Arnold Ventures, The Audacious Project, Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, John and Patrick Collison, Crankstart, Jack Dorsey, Kim and Scott Farquhar, Paul Graham, Reid Hoffman, Fiona McKean and Tobias Lütke, Yuri and Julia Milner, Elon Musk, Crystal and Chris Sacca, Schmidt Futures, and (August 2020) MacKenzie Scott. In their retrospective "What We Learned Doing Fast Grants," the organizers noted that many donors made seven-figure gifts "without ever speaking with us." Fast Grants was the explicit "dry run" that convinced Konermann and Hsu the fast-granting model worked, leading directly to Arc.

**Progress Studies.** Coined in the July 2019 Atlantic essay "We Need a New Science of Progress" by Patrick Collison and Tyler Cowen — itself a sequel to the November 2018 Collison–Michael Nielsen Atlantic piece "Science Is Getting Less Bang for Its Buck," which used a Nobel-discovery survey to argue that per-dollar scientific productivity has fallen. The 2019 essay argued Progress Studies should be "closer to medicine than biology: the goal is to treat, not merely to understand." It spawned an ecosystem: the **Roots of Progress Institute**, the **Institute for Progress**, and **Works in Progress** — and prompted Ezra Klein's "supply-side progressivism" and the later Abundance movement.

**Roots of Progress Institute (RPI).** Founded by Jason Crawford, who began the blog in 2017 and went full-time via a 2019 Emergent Ventures grant; incorporated as a 501(c)(3) (tax-exempt since October 2021) and rebranded to "Roots of Progress Institute" June 25, 2024. Its mission (per IRS filing) is "to define and communicate a new philosophy of progress for the twenty-first century." It runs a multi-year Fellowship for "progress writers," the Progress Forum/Slack, and the annual Progress Conference. FY2024 revenue was ~$1.9M (96% contributions); Crawford's compensation was $200,000. Funders include the Mercatus Center and Open Philanthropy/Coefficient Giving.

**The Progress Conference.** First held October 18–19, 2024 in Berkeley at the Lighthaven campus (the rationalist venue), themed "Toward Abundant Futures," co-hosted by RPI with the Foresight Institute, HumanProgress.org, the Institute for Humane Studies, the **Institute for Progress**, and Works in Progress. 2024 keynotes: Patrick Collison, Tyler Cowen, Jason Crawford, and Steven Pinker, plus a Dwarkesh Patel × Patrick Collison conversation. IFP separately ran an East Coast "Abundance 2024" conference in Washington, D.C. Progress Conference 2025 (Oct 16–19, 2025) grew to 350+ attendees with keynotes from Sam Altman, Tyler Cowen, Jennifer Pahlka, and Blake Scholl; 2026 is scheduled for October.

**Metascience funding infrastructure.** Patrick Collison sits on the advisory committee of J-PAL's Science for Progress Initiative (alongside Matt Clancy and Emily Oehlsen of Open Phil, Cowen, Kumar Garg of Schmidt Futures, and Caleb Watney of IFP). The intellectual scaffolding runs through Michael Nielsen's metascience essays (including "A Vision of Metascience" with Kanjun Qiu) and is adjacent to Convergent Research/Schmidt Futures' Focused Research Organizations (FROs), Tom Kalil's Renaissance Philanthropy, and the UK's ARIA.

### Part 3 — The Wider Network and People Map

The defining structural fact is that **the Collison orbit and the Moskovitz/Tuna (Good Ventures/Open Philanthropy) orbit have effectively merged at the funding layer.** The Arc donor list, the Fast Grants donor list, and the Abundance & Growth Fund donor list overlap heavily.

**Central people.** Tyler Cowen is the intellectual hub — Mercatus Center director, founder of Emergent Ventures (which seeded Roots of Progress and Works in Progress), author of Marginal Revolution and host of Conversations with Tyler, and Patrick's co-author and co-founder on Fast Grants and Progress Studies. Patrick Hsu and Silvana Konermann are the scientific core. Michael Nielsen is the metascience intellectual. Jason Crawford runs the movement's philosophy shop. The Works in Progress founding editors — Sam Bowman (also on Mercatus's board and now Stripe's Head of Publishing), Saloni Dattani, Ben Southwood, and Nick Whitaker — anchor the London node.

**Nat Friedman and Daniel Gross** are recurring co-funders: with the Collisons they back both the **Vesuvius Challenge** and Leopold Aschenbrenner's hedge fund. The **Vesuvius Challenge** (launched March 15, 2023 by Friedman, Gross, and University of Kentucky computer scientist Brent Seales) offered $1M+ in prizes (from 30+ donors giving $5K–$450K) to read the carbonized Herculaneum scrolls with AI; the 2023 Grand Prize ($700,000) was won in February 2024 by Youssef Nader, Luke Farritor, and Julian Schilliger, and by 2025 an entire scroll (PHerc 1667) had been read end-to-end — arguably the movement's most romantic success and a template for prize-based open science.

**AI investing.** Both brothers are backers of **Situational Awareness LP**, the AI-focused hedge fund founded by former OpenAI Superalignment researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner (other backers: Nat Friedman and Daniel Gross). Per the Wall Street Journal (June 8, 2026), the fund surpassed $20 billion in AUM, is "up more than 1,000% after fees since its launch" (and ~270% in 2026 through May), now counts Jane Street as an investor, and holds Anthropic (first bought February 2025 above a $60B valuation) at roughly 20% of assets. Patrick has made 50+ angel investments (PitchBook lists 57), skewed toward "infrastructure" and "science-AI"; he sits on the board of nuclear-fusion startup Pacific Fusion and advises the Stellar Development Foundation. The brothers also co-invested in Retool. Patrick sits on Meta's board. Notably, the Collisons' documented AI exposure is more *accelerationist-adjacent* (Aschenbrenner, Anthropic via the fund) than AI-safety-native, though the Anthropic tilt and the EA-world funding ties place them at an interesting midpoint between the accelerationist and safety camps.

**Links back to Open Phil / EA.** Patrick Collison is a named contributor to Coefficient Giving's (formerly Open Philanthropy) $120M Abundance & Growth Fund (announced March 2025) — per Coefficient's own words: "We're grateful for support from Good Ventures, which has committed $60M, and from the other private funders who matched them. We're also grateful for a contribution from Patrick Collison, who helped launch the Progress Studies movement." Per Forbes (Phoebe Liu, Nov 18, 2025), "New billionaire funders include Stripe cofounder Patrick Collison, Lucy Southworth (Google cofounder Larry Page's wife) and Bill Gates," who helped seed both the Lead Exposure Action Fund and the Abundance & Growth Fund; more than $200M in 2025 commitments to Coefficient came from funders other than Good Ventures. The **Institute for Progress** (co-founded 2022 by Alec Stapp and Caleb Watney) was funded by Open Philanthropy (a $1.6M+ grant, later multi-year support), Emergent Ventures, both Collisons, and — historically — Sam Bankman-Fried/Future Fund. Arc's founding donors literally include Dustin Moskovitz and Cari Tuna, the Good Ventures principals. The relationship is thus both financial (co-funded funds and organizations) and personal.

**Intellectual tension with EA.** Progress Studies and abundance sit in productive tension with EA longtermism: where longtermism emphasizes existential risk and (in its AI-safety strand) sometimes caution about acceleration, Progress Studies emphasizes growth, technological optimism, and *speeding up*. The merger of funding streams (Open Phil now runs an explicit Abundance & Growth Fund and directs $100M+/year to AI safety simultaneously) shows the two camps have found a modus vivendi rather than a clean synthesis.

### Part 4 — The Europe Angle

**Ireland.** Stripe maintains a genuine dual HQ in Dublin, and the Collisons remain culturally and now politically engaged with Ireland. In 2024 they spearheaded **Progress Ireland**, a think tank led by CEO Sean Keyes (a former finance correspondent at The Currency), with directors including Luke Fehily, Sean O'Neill McPartlin (co-founder of the Better Planning Alliance), and Sean Keyes; its focus is public policy, housing, infrastructure, and visa reform. In an October 2025 Irish Times essay, John Collison argued that Irish construction and infrastructure are throttled by regulation and judicial review stemming from the 1980s–90s planning-corruption scandals. The intervention became a political event: Tánaiste Simon Harris praised it as "thought-provoking," Fianna Fáil TD Malcolm Byrne sought to invite Collison to address his parliamentary party, and Fine Gael's James Geoghegan said Ireland has "world-class talent" but "third-world delivery." UCD's Orla Hegarty pushed back sharply: "It jumps to a solution that doesn't relate to the problem… That's a really naive take." John has also criticized Ireland's slow work-visa system. The brothers' Irish giving includes University of Limerick ties and Young Scientist connections; note that a 2021 Forbes profile describing Limerick as a "warzone" drew a public backlash and rebukes from both brothers ("daft," per John).

**UK / European progress ecosystem.** Works in Progress is London-based, and its editors are embedded in the UK YIMBY/planning-reform world (Ben Southwood via Create Streets and Policy Exchange; Sam Bowman via the free-market think-tank scene). **ARIA** — the UK's DARPA-style Advanced Research and Invention Agency, the brainchild of Dominic Cummings, established by statute in 2022 and operational from January 2023 — shares clear intellectual DNA with the metascience movement: Cummings named Michael Nielsen and mathematician Tim Gowers as potential ARIA leaders, and the "high-risk, high-reward, low-bureaucracy" ethos mirrors Fast Grants/Arc. (ARIA is separately controversial: a 2025 Guardian/Democracy for Sale investigation found it channeled ~£52M to US firms, some backed by Silicon Valley billionaires including a Sam Altman-backed company.) Patrick Collison's personal site flags Europe as "currently off-track, falling behind the US in key technologies and general prosperity" and praises both Progress Ireland and the Institute for Progress — a signal of likely continued European engagement.

### Part 5 — Criticisms and Assessment

**The left critique (deregulation / billionaire capture).** The sharpest sourced material is the American Prospect's June 12, 2026 exposé, "New Documents Detail Nine-Figure, Silicon Valley–Funded Abundance Movement" (Revolving Door Project), which published leaked fundraising documents describing an "Abundance Capital Stack" of roughly "$260 million annually" from billionaire benefactors and quoted a memo stating CA YIMBY's funding "largely originates from 'tech founders: Mark Zuckerberg, Dustin Moskowitz, Patrick Collison, Ken Duda [who] are 80%+ of the funding.'" It also quoted Abundance Network founder Zack Rosen's striking anti-populist line that "small dollar internet fundraising makes politics dumber." The Prospect (Dylan Gyauch-Lewis), Jacobin ("Abundance is neoliberalism repackaged"), People's Policy Project (Matt Bruenig), and The Nation frame the movement as a rebranded neoliberal deregulation project that offers "shelter to effective altruists" after FTX and risks acting as "a technocratic buffer to prevent more transformative reforms." Aaron Timms (LARB/New Republic) attacks the underlying "engineerialism… rule by experts remarketed for the innovation age."

**Academic skepticism of the core thesis.** The "science is slowing / ideas are getting harder to find" claim — the intellectual foundation of the whole enterprise, drawing on Bloom, Jones, Van Reenen & Webb (AER 2020) and the Collison–Nielsen survey — is genuinely contested. Metascience writer Alexey Guzey argues "Bloom et al switch the way they define 'ideas' without any apparent principle, from case study to case study," undermining comparability; agricultural economists dispute the metric choice (constant research effort still delivered constant absolute crop-yield gains); and physicist Chad Orzel called the Nobel-survey methodology "unconvincing." Even Patrick Collison, pressed on Open Philanthropy's "Metascience 101" podcast, conceded he meant science could be perhaps "25% better than today" — "a relatively small improvement" — not that a 20x productivity decline could be reversed.

**Assessment of the science itself.** Critiques of Arc and Fast Grants are notably thinner than critiques of the political project — most coverage is favorable (real *Nature*/*Science* papers, open models, a working saliva COVID test). The substantive concerns are (1) governance — the Chronicle of Philanthropy captured scientist unease that "science projects for the public good are driven by a handful of tech elites motivated by the 'move fast and break things' ethos," with Purdue sociologist David Peterson saying such approaches "may be useful in a circumscribed way" — and (2) the counterfactual problem: it is genuinely hard to prove that Arc/Fast Grants outputs "wouldn't have happened" via conventional funding, a limitation acknowledged even within the movement. There is no substantial published finding that Arc has *failed* to deliver.

**Stripe controversies.** The 2022 layoffs and the 2023 down-round (from $95B to ~$50B) were real setbacks the founders owned publicly; the aggressive 2024–25 crypto/stablecoin pivot (Bridge, Tempo) is a large, still-unproven bet that some observers view as chasing a "$0 market" — a critique John Collison himself has gestured at internally ("I don't think crypto… merited the investment it got based on the number of customer requests").

**Bottom line: substance vs. brand.** The honest verdict is that this ecosystem is *disproportionately* substantive for a founder-vanity operation — Arc's science, Frontier's deployments, the Vesuvius decipherment, and Works in Progress's essays are real contributions — but its *political* arm (abundance/deregulation) is a genuine ideological project with genuine opponents, and its self-narrative ("science is broken, we can fix it") rests on empirical claims that remain contested. The brand and the substance are entangled, not separable.

### Part 6 — Current State (2025–2026) and Trajectory

As of mid-2026: Stripe is at $159B and explicitly avoiding an IPO, betting the next decade on agentic commerce and stablecoins (Bridge, Tempo, Privy). Arc is scaling its Virtual Cell Initiative and Evo line and is the clearest "substance test" to watch. **Frontier** committed "an additional $915 million" (buyers including Stripe, Google, Shopify, Salesforce, H&M Group, and — its first AI-startup backer — Anthropic), "taking Frontier's total commitment to $1.8 billion"; in 2025 seven portfolio companies delivered ~23,000 tons (~2x the prior year) and broke ground on 1.4M tons of new annual capacity. The Coefficient Giving Abundance & Growth Fund is the money-flow to watch on the policy side. Progress Ireland is the leading signal of European expansion, and Patrick's stated preoccupation with European decline suggests more to come. The trajectory is toward *deeper* fusion of the Collison and Open Phil/EA worlds (shared funds, shared personnel, shared conferences), while the external political environment grows more adversarial as the abundance movement becomes a named target of the American left.

## Recommendations
- **For tracking substance vs. hype:** Watch Arc Institute's virtual-cell and Evo outputs (peer-reviewed papers, external adoption) as the cleanest test of whether the "new model of science" produces durable results. A benchmark that would update you positively: an Arc model or tool driving a clinical or drug-discovery result attributable specifically to Arc. A negative signal: continued high-profile model releases without downstream biological validation.
- **For tracking the money and politics:** Monitor the Coefficient Giving Abundance & Growth Fund's grantees and the Progress/Abundance conference rosters — these reveal where Collison-aligned capital is flowing. The threshold that would change the picture: whether the abundance agenda converts into enacted federal legislation (permitting/NEPA, immigration, science-funding reform) versus remaining think-tank discourse.
- **For the Europe watch:** Treat Progress Ireland and any UK/EU science-funding or planning initiative as the leading indicators of whether the Collisons export their model. A concrete Irish planning-law change credited to the Collison intervention would be the clearest signal.
- **Epistemic posture:** Treat "science is slowing" and "deregulation drives abundance" as *contested hypotheses the ecosystem is built on*, not settled facts — weight the movement's outputs accordingly.

## Caveats
- **Net worth figures diverge materially:** Forbes 2026 puts each brother at ~$17.5B; Bloomberg estimates John nearer ~$9.3B. Both are model-based estimates of illiquid private stock.
- **"Cheque Depot" is a misnomer** — John Collison's podcast is "A Cheeky Pint" (2025–). No property called "Cheque Depot" exists.
- **The famous Cowen interview was by Patrick, not John**, and a John Collison interview with Summers or Rogoff could not be verified.
- **Political-donation and citizenship details are lower-confidence:** Reporting (Irish Times, Business Post) indicates Patrick shifted toward Republican donations (~$200K) ahead of 2024 while historically giving to Democrats since 2016; the Collisons are Irish citizens, and the legality/mechanics of their US political giving depend on their immigration status, which is not fully public. Treat specific dollar figures here as indicative.
- **Some ecosystem financials rely on secondary sources** (Grokipedia, Influence Watch, SEO profiles) where primary filings were unavailable; these are flagged in-text and should be treated as indicative rather than definitive.
- This report deliberately does not re-derive the Good Ventures/Open Philanthropy/Coefficient Giving internal structure (covered in the prior two reports); it maps only the connective tissue to the Collison world.
