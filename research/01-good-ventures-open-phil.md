# Good Ventures & Open Philanthropy (Coefficient Giving): A Network Map and Institutional History

## TL;DR
- **Good Ventures** — the private foundation of Facebook/Asana co-founder Dustin Moskovitz and former Wall Street Journal reporter Cari Tuna — holds roughly $10 billion and is the anchor donor behind **Open Philanthropy**, which rebranded to **Coefficient Giving** on November 18, 2025; together they have "directed more than $4 billion in philanthropic funds" since 2014 and function as the single largest funder of effective altruism, farm-animal welfare, and AI safety worldwide.
- The network's defining feature is dense interlock: OP co-founder **Holden Karnofsky** is married to Anthropic president **Daniela Amodei** and joined Anthropic in January 2025; Moskovitz and Skype's Jaan Tallinn were early Anthropic investors; and OP alumni populate CSET, RAND, the US government's AI-policy apparatus, OpenAI's former board, Anthropic, and METR — making OP simultaneously a philanthropic funder and a talent pipeline into the AI industry and government.
- The 2024 Good Ventures decision to exit certain sub-causes (rationalist orgs, digital-minds work, right-of-center think tanks, high-school outreach) exposed the structural fragility of an ecosystem dependent on one donor, and the network faces sustained criticism from the left (billionaire influence), the right (Cruz/Politico "AI doomer" lobbying probes), and from within EA (insularity and conflict-of-interest concerns amplified by the FTX collapse).

## Key Findings

**1. Structure.** Good Ventures has no full-time staff; it is a grantmaking shell that acts on recommendations from Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy) and GiveWell. It comprises the Good Ventures Foundation (a private foundation) and Good Ventures LLC (a for-profit impact-investment vehicle that donates net earnings to the foundation). Coefficient Giving is the research-and-grantmaking engine.

**2. Lineage.** GiveWell (2007, Karnofsky & Elie Hassenfeld, ex-Bridgewater) → GiveWell Labs (2011, partnership with Good Ventures) → Open Philanthropy Project (2014) → independent LLC spin-out (2017) → "Open Philanthropy" (2019) → "Coefficient Giving" (2025).

**3. People.** Alexander Berger is sole CEO (co-CEO from 2021, sole since ~2023); Cari Tuna is chair. Karnofsky departed for AI-strategy work, then Anthropic. A large alumni diaspora now sits at Anthropic, METR, CSET, RAND, NIST, Forethought, Redwood Research, and in government.

**4. Money flows into the AI industry and government.** OP made a $30M grant to OpenAI in 2017 (buying a board seat for Karnofsky); funded CSET at Georgetown with $55M (2019); created and funded the Horizon Institute for Public Service, which places fellows in Congress and federal agencies.

**5. Controversy.** The FTX collapse (Nov 2022), the 2024 cause-exits, the Politico/Cruz "billionaire-backed AI network" investigations, and long-running EA debates about insularity and board interlocks all shape the network's reputation in 2025-2026.

## Details

### PART 1 — GOOD VENTURES

**Founding and structure.** Cari Tuna (born October 4, 1985, in Minnesota; raised in Evansville, Indiana; Yale political science, 2008) was a corporate/tech reporter at the Wall Street Journal's San Francisco bureau when she began dating Dustin Moskovitz in 2009. In 2010 the couple signed the Giving Pledge — at 25, Tuna became the youngest-ever signatory. They launched Good Ventures in 2011; Tuna quit the Journal to run it full-time, and married Moskovitz in October 2013. Good Ventures comprises two entities created in 2011-2012: the Good Ventures Foundation (a private charity) and Good Ventures LLC (a for-profit impact-investment firm that donates net earnings to the foundation). The foundation has no employees and relies entirely on Coefficient Giving and GiveWell for research and grant recommendations. As of 2025 it holds roughly $10 billion in assets (Forbes, Nov 18, 2025: "the Good Ventures Foundation—Tuna and Moskovitz's private foundation with $10 billion in assets—has primarily funded Coefficient Giving") and, per various sources, has distributed between $3 billion and $5 billion cumulatively since 2011. In 2024 Good Ventures made 493 grants (median $115,000; range $367 to $31 million). Tuna and Moskovitz were named to the inaugural TIME100 Philanthropy list in 2025.

**Dustin Moskovitz.** Born May 22, 1984, in Gainesville, Florida; co-founded Facebook with Mark Zuckerberg in 2004; left in 2008 to co-found Asana with Justin Rosenstein. In March 2011 Forbes named him the world's youngest self-made billionaire. His net worth is volatile and source-dependent: Forbes listed roughly $17.4 billion in May 2025, falling to about $10.2 billion by April 2026; in May 2025 Bloomberg removed his Meta stake from its estimate (cutting ~$18 billion) because filings could no longer confirm ownership. The Nov 2025 Forbes rebrand piece put the couple's combined wealth at "some $20 billion." Karnofsky has cautioned that public net-worth figures understate available philanthropic resources because much is already inside Good Ventures. On March 10, 2025, Asana announced Moskovitz would step down as CEO to become board chair; Dan Rogers (ex-LaunchDarkly) became CEO effective July 21, 2025. Moskovitz said he "feel[s] called to dedicate more of my energy to addressing global challenges through Good Ventures and Open Philanthropy." He is a major Democratic donor: ~$20M+ for Clinton in 2016, ~$24M for Biden in 2020, and for 2024 he gave $10M to Future Forward PAC plus $38M via Asana in support of Kamala Harris, making Asana the largest non-PAC donor.

**Cari Tuna.** President of Good Ventures and chair of Coefficient Giving; also serves on GiveWell's board. She frames cause selection around "importance, neglectedness, and tractability."

**The 2024 cause-exit controversy.** In June 2024, Good Ventures announced it would stop supporting certain sub-causes (amounting to "less than 5% of our annual grantmaking") and would no longer expand into new causes by default. Neither Good Ventures nor OP published a list, which fueled community anxiety. Per Benjamin Todd's January 2025 summary and OP staff comments, the exited/avoided areas included: organizations associated with the **rationalist community** (e.g., LessWrong, MIRI), **digital minds / AI moral-patienthood** research, many **right-of-center (Republican-leaning) think tanks**, **high-school outreach** programs (e.g., Non-Trivial faced a funding gap), and some non-US think tanks. Alexander Berger apologized "to directly affected grantees…for the negative surprise." Moskovitz defended the move, citing concern about OP's "attack surface" and his rejection of "rationalist-level decoupling." By October 1, 2025, Good Ventures had partially reversed course: it became open again to funding digital-minds/AI-moral-patienthood work and to considering a wider range of right-of-center AI policy and macrostrategy/futurism grants case-by-case. The episode became the movement's canonical example of "structural risk" from single-funder dependency.

### PART 2 — OPEN PHILANTHROPY / COEFFICIENT GIVING: INSTITUTIONAL HISTORY & PEOPLE

**Origins.** Holden Karnofsky and Elie Hassenfeld met at Bridgewater Associates, started a workplace charity-evaluation club in 2006, formed "The Clear Fund," and quit to found GiveWell in 2007. Good Ventures and GiveWell announced a close partnership in June 2012; the collaboration produced GiveWell Labs (2011) for more speculative giving, renamed the Open Philanthropy Project in 2014, spun out as an independent LLC in 2017, shortened to "Open Philanthropy" in 2019, and rebranded to Coefficient Giving on November 18, 2025.

**The rebrand.** CEO Alexander Berger explained that "Open Philanthropy" was frequently confused with OpenAI and the Open Society Foundations, and that the org's growing multi-donor work no longer matched a name synonymous with Good Ventures. A naming agency proposed "Coefficient Giving" ("co" = collaboration; "efficient" = cost-effectiveness; a coefficient multiplies value). Per Coefficient Giving's own Nov 18, 2025 essay: "In 2024, we directed over $100 million to high-impact causes from donors besides Good Ventures. In 2025 so far, that has more than doubled, putting our non-Good Ventures work on par with that of some other major philanthropic organizations." It directed over $1 billion total in 2025 ("the most in our history"). Programs are being converted into 13 multi-donor funds (minimum ~$250,000/year to join), including the $125M Lead Exposure Action Fund (with the Gates Foundation and Lucy Southworth) and the $120M Abundance & Growth Fund (with Stripe's Patrick Collison). Bill Gates endorsed the change. The press release stated: "Since its founding, Coefficient Giving has directed more than $4 billion in philanthropic funds, working closely with Good Ventures," with Berger quoted: "We've spent the last decade building a model for giving effectively at scale."

**Leadership and key people:**
- **Alexander Berger** — co-founder; ran Global Health & Wellbeing; co-CEO 2021, sole CEO since ~2023. Stanford philosophy + education policy; former GiveWell researcher; notable non-directed-kidney donor.
- **Cari Tuna** — chair.
- **Holden Karnofsky** — co-founder, CEO then co-CEO until 2023; Director of AI Strategy; Carnegie Endowment visiting scholar (2024); joined Anthropic January 2025 as a member of technical staff working on the Responsible Scaling Policy, reporting to CSO Jared Kaplan. Married to Anthropic president Daniela Amodei (August 2017); one son, Galileo; formerly roommate of Dario Amodei. Served on OpenAI's board 2017-2021.
- **Elie Hassenfeld** — GiveWell co-founder and CEO.
- **Lewis Bollard** — farm animal welfare lead; OP is "the world's biggest funder of farm animal welfare."
- **Ajeya Cotra** — author of the 2020 "biological anchors" transformative-AI-timelines report; led OP's technical AI safety program in 2024; departed to **METR** (loss-of-control risk assessment).
- **Luke Muehlhauser** — former MIRI executive director (2011-2015); joined OP 2015; now Program Director for AI governance/policy at Coefficient Giving. Was on Anthropic's board January 2021 – May 2024, resigning May 28, 2024, citing the complication of being an AI-governance grantmaker while sitting on a frontier AI company's board (he held no Anthropic equity); he stated the resignation was not safety-related.
- **Claire Zabel** — ran Global Catastrophic Risks Capacity Building (2018-2024); now Program Director leading the "AI Short Timelines Special Projects" team.
- **Nick Beckstead** — early OP program officer (global catastrophic risk); left to become CEO of the FTX Foundation and run the FTX Future Fund (Nov 2021-Nov 2022); later Policy Lead at the Center for AI Safety; now co-founder/CEO of Secure AI Project.

**Alumni diaspora.** OP has become a talent pipeline into the AI industry, think tanks, and government:
- **Helen Toner** — former OP senior AI-policy analyst → co-founded CSET → OpenAI board member who voted to remove Sam Altman (Nov 17, 2023) and left the board days later; now interim Executive Director of CSET (August 2025).
- **Jason Matheny** — founding director of OP-funded CSET → IARPA → Biden White House (Deputy Assistant to the President for Technology & National Security; OSTP/NSC, 2021-2022) → President & CEO of RAND Corporation (July 2022). Briefly on Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust, stepping down December 2023 for conflict reasons.
- **Paul Christiano** (RLHF pioneer, tied to OP-funded alignment work; Ajeya Cotra's partner) → Head of AI Safety at the US AI Safety Institute (NIST), April 2024; his appointment drew internal NIST objections over EA ties.
- **Joe Carlsmith** — OP senior research analyst → Anthropic ("Leaving Open Philanthropy, going to Anthropic").
- **Lukas Finnveden** → Redwood Research (2025). **Tom Davidson** → Forethought (2025).
- **Chloe Cockburn** — OP criminal-justice program officer; OP spun off the program into Just Impact (~$50M, 2021); she now runs Just Impact Advisors.

### PART 3 — ECOSYSTEM LINKS MAP

**Effective altruism.** OP is described as "the single biggest funder" of the EA movement, having multiplied annual EA donations by roughly 100x. It funds (directly or via regranting) the Centre for Effective Altruism / Effective Ventures, 80,000 Hours, Giving What We Can, EA Funds (the Long-Term Future Fund and EA Infrastructure Fund), and community-building globally. OP's own program officers note that OP is "the largest funder for most of our grantees, leading to a relatively high dependency," and that "most grantees would likely need to scale down or close operations entirely without our support." Nick Beckstead originally managed the EA Funds' Long-Term Future and EA Community funds while at OP.

**The FTX/SBF collapse (Nov 2022).** OP has stated it "never received any donations from FTX, the Future Fund, or any of the individuals who worked there." But the collapse hit OP directly: its "available assets fell by half and then recovered half their losses," and the FTX Future Fund "collapsed suddenly and left a sizable funding gap" in biosecurity and AI-risk areas. OP launched an application process to support stranded Future Fund grantees within its longtermist focus areas. Karnofsky wrote that it was "very likely that FTX engaged in outrageous, unacceptable fraud." Moskovitz tweeted: "Either EA encouraged Sam's unethical behavior, or provided convenient rationalization for such actions. Either is bad." The episode dealt a reputational blow to EA and prompted EA Forum calls for an independent investigation into what leaders knew.

**AI industry.**
- **OpenAI:** OP recommended a $30M grant ($10M/year for 3 years) to OpenAI in 2017, explicitly to buy a board seat and partnership rather than to scale OpenAI. OP's own grant writeup states: "We expect the primary benefits of this grant to stem from our partnership with OpenAI, rather than simply from contributing funding toward OpenAI's work…particularly the opportunity to help play a role in OpenAI's approach to safety and governance issues." Karnofsky took the seat (2017-2021), then resigned citing conflict (his wife co-founding Anthropic) and was succeeded by Helen Toner. OP later assessed the grant's counterfactual impact on OpenAI's existence as near-zero.
- **Anthropic:** The three biggest EA financial backers — Moskovitz, Jaan Tallinn, and Sam Bankman-Fried — were all early Anthropic investors. Per Forge Data, "The Anthropic Series A took place in May 2021, when the company raised $124.62 million at a post-money valuation of $623.11 million"; Anthropic's own release names participants "James McClave, Dustin Moskovitz, the Center for Emerging Risk Research, Eric Schmidt, and others," with Tallinn leading the round. SBF/FTX later bought ~$500M of non-voting shares in the 2022 Series B. The Karnofsky-Amodei marriage and the Muehlhauser board seat deepened the interlock. Anthropic reached a reported ~$380B valuation (Series G, Feb 2026). The FTX estate's Anthropic stake was sold (reported ~$884M) to repay creditors.
- **AI safety field-building:** OP funds Redwood Research, ARC/METR, MIRI (historically), CHAI, the Center for AI Safety, GovAI, and CSET. Coefficient's technical AI-safety grantmaking scaled from ~$40M (2024) to over $140M (2025) as the team tripled headcount.

**Policy/government.**
- **CSET:** Per OP's grant page and Philanthropy News Digest, "The Open Philanthropy Project recommended a grant of $55,000,000 over five years to Georgetown University to launch the Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET)" in 2019. A later general-support grant plus $8M lifted total OP funding to more than $100 million from 2019 to 2025 (per CSET director Dewey Murdick). CSET is led by Jason Matheny and staffed partly by OP alumni (Helen Toner as director of strategy).
- **Horizon Institute for Public Service:** effectively created and initially funded by OP. Per Politico (Brendan Bordelon, Oct 13, 2023, "How a Billionaire-Backed Network of AI Advisers Took Over Washington"): "In 2022, Open Philanthropy set aside nearly $3 million to pay for what ultimately became the initial cohort of Horizon fellows," funding "more than a dozen AI fellows in key congressional offices, across federal agencies and at influential think tanks." Good Ventures gave Horizon $11.8M in 2023. Horizon places fellows ($113,000/year) in Congress, the executive branch (State, Commerce, HHS, DoD, White House/NSC), national labs, and think tanks (Brookings, RAND, Carnegie, CSIS), with a 100% placement rate and 50+ fellows placed.
- **Biden AI Executive Order (14110, Oct 2023):** RAND (under Matheny) provided technical analysis; critics alleged OP/Moskovitz-funded fellows placed via the Intergovernmental Personnel Act were shaping policy from inside agencies. Senator Ted Cruz's July 1, 2024 Senate Commerce Committee letter charged that Moskovitz "is paying the salaries of his fellows serving in AI roles at the National Security Council, the Department of Defense, and the Department of Commerce…large AI technology companies are influencing the Biden administration's AI policy from the inside and advancing their own anti-competitive agenda." RAND countered that Matheny had "no role" drafting the EO, saying its "researchers did provide technical expertise and analysis to inform the EO in response to requests from policymakers."

**Other donor networks.**
- **Jaan Tallinn** (Skype co-founder) — co-funder across AI safety; runs the Survival and Flourishing Fund (S-process), which shares many grantees with OP.
- **Navigation Fund** (Jed McCaleb, ~$1.3B, chaired by Seemay Chou; president David Coman-Hidy) — a newer public charity funding farm-animal welfare, AI safety, criminal justice, open science, climate, and digital sentience; scaled from ~$4M (2023) to $60M+ (2025); it has stepped into some areas Good Ventures exited (e.g., shrimp/wild-animal welfare). Its asset base is tied to Voltage Park (~$500M in Nvidia H100s).
- **Longview Philanthropy** and **Founders Pledge** — advisory/regranting partners in AI safety and global catastrophic risk; OP directs donors giving $250k+/year to Longview.
- **Gates Foundation** (Lead Exposure Action Fund co-funder), **Patrick Collison/Stripe** (Abundance & Growth Fund co-funder), plus adjacency to the progress-studies/abundance movement (Emergent Ventures/Tyler Cowen) — situated in the map but covered in the prior report.
- Billionaire-donor orbit also includes Sam Altman, Vitalik Buterin, and Eric Schmidt (Anthropic Series A), reflecting overlapping AI-safety and rationalist funding networks.

**Media/intellectual infrastructure.** Vox's **Future Perfect** (launched 2018 by Dylan Matthews under Ezra Klein) was seeded by a $380,000 Rockefeller Foundation grant; Kelsey Piper stated Future Perfect "probably would not take funding from…the Open Philanthropy Project directly," to preserve editorial independence — though OP funds much of the surrounding EA media ecosystem. **Asterisk** magazine (Clara Collier, editor) sits in the EA/rationalist-adjacent intellectual orbit, as does Works in Progress. Journalists and intellectuals in the orbit include Dylan Matthews, Kelsey Piper, and (via Vox founding) Ezra Klein connections. Separately, the Tarbell Center for AI Journalism (EA-adjacent) embeds fellows in mainstream newsrooms, which became a 2025 flashpoint in accelerationist-vs-safety media battles (Semafor, Dec 2025).

### PART 4 — CONTROVERSIES & CRITICISMS

- **OpenAI grant retrospective:** EA-internal debate over whether the $30M grant validated/accelerated OpenAI; OP's own retrospective judged the counterfactual effect on OpenAI's existence near-zero, but critics see reputational cost.
- **Board interlocks / conflicts of interest:** the Karnofsky-Amodei marriage, the Muehlhauser Anthropic board seat, Toner's move from OP to CSET to OpenAI's board, and OP's link to Anthropic (a major OpenAI competitor) recur in critiques of insularity.
- **2024 Good Ventures exits:** criticized inside EA as one funder's judgment shaping an entire field; the opacity (no published list) drew particular ire.
- **Worm wars:** the long-running deworming-effectiveness dispute; GiveWell (with OP funding of Cochrane) repeatedly reaffirmed deworming's cost-effectiveness while acknowledging deep uncertainty; in 2022 GiveWell removed four deworming programs from its top-charities list.
- **Political vehicles:** the Open Philanthropy Action Fund (501(c)(4) political arm, criminal-justice and other advocacy); Moskovitz's Future Forward PAC and ballot-measure/YIMBY giving.
- **External critiques:** from the left, concern about outsized billionaire influence over public policy; from the right, the Cruz/Politico/Washington Examiner framing of an "effective altruism"–linked "AI doomer" lobby capturing federal AI policy.

### PART 5 — WHAT THE NETWORK MEANS TODAY (2025-2026)

Coefficient Giving is deliberately repositioning from "Dustin and Cari's thing" into a multi-donor platform, courting a new generation of tech wealth (explicitly anticipating an Anthropic IPO that could mint a wave of EA-aligned donors). It directed over $1 billion in 2025 and is the dominant global funder of AI safety. Yet the same interlock that gives the network reach — marriage and board ties to Anthropic, alumni across CSET/RAND/NIST/OpenAI, and a fellowship pipeline into Congress and federal agencies — is precisely what fuels both conflict-of-interest critiques and partisan investigations. The 2024 cause-exits demonstrated that single-donor dependency remains the ecosystem's core vulnerability, only partially mitigated by the arrival of the Navigation Fund and Coefficient's multi-donor pivot.

## Recommendations

- **For funders/organizations dependent on OP/Good Ventures:** treat single-funder reliance as an active risk. Benchmark: if more than ~50% of your budget comes from Coefficient/Good Ventures, prioritize diversification toward the Navigation Fund, Longview, Founders Pledge, SFF, or EA Funds. The 2024 exits (and partial October 2025 reversal) show cause-area inclusion can change with little warning.
- **For journalists/researchers mapping influence:** focus on the interlock nodes — the Karnofsky-Amodei marriage, the OP→CSET→OpenAI-board chain (Toner), OP→RAND/NIST/White House (Matheny, Christiano), and the Horizon fellowship-to-agency pipeline. These are where funding, industry, and government most directly connect. Threshold for concern: named individuals simultaneously holding grantmaking authority and industry/government decision roles.
- **For observers of the AI-safety funding landscape:** watch the anticipated Anthropic IPO as the single biggest potential shifter — it could create the largest concentration of longtermist philanthropic capital in history (via founder and early-investor equity) and reduce the field's dependence on Good Ventures. Benchmark: whether non-Good-Ventures giving through Coefficient continues doubling year-over-year (it exceeded $200M in 2025, up from $100M+ in 2024).
- **For assessing the network's political exposure:** monitor whether Congressional investigations (Cruz) and Politico-style coverage translate into legal/regulatory constraints on IPA-funded fellowships or 501(c)(4) advocacy. A change in administration posture toward EA-aligned AI policy would materially alter the network's government footprint.

## Caveats

- **Net-worth figures are volatile and source-dependent.** Moskovitz's estimated wealth ranges from ~$6.8B (FTX-era low, 2022) to ~$17.4B (Forbes, May 2025) to ~$10.2B (April 2026); Bloomberg removed his Meta stake in 2025. OP itself argues headline figures understate philanthropic resources already parked in Good Ventures. Cumulative "distributed" grant figures ($3B/$4B/$5B) vary by source and by whether one counts "directed" vs. "distributed."
- **Some relationships are inference, not confirmed fact.** The precise causal weight of OP-funded fellows on specific policy outcomes (e.g., the Biden AI EO) is contested; RAND says Matheny had "no role" drafting the EO while critics claim influence. Partisan sources (Fox News, Washington Examiner, Cruz Commerce Committee) are flagged as such.
- **A few personnel details could not be fully verified** within research limits (e.g., any OP staff role for Tom Kalil, whose documented path runs through Schmidt Futures/Renaissance Philanthropy, not OP; and the status of Heather Youngs). These are noted rather than asserted.
- **Lower-tier and AI-generated sources** (Grokipedia, LongtermWiki, net-worth aggregators, some crypto outlets) were used only where corroborated by primary or reputable secondary sources; the FTX-Anthropic stake sale figure varies ($884M vs. $1.34B) across outlets.
- **Forward-looking items** (Anthropic IPO, future giving levels) are explicitly speculative and drawn from commentators' projections, not established facts.
